About me.

Hi, I’m a PhD candidate in the Plant Ecology and Remote Sensing Lab, advised by Prof. Xi Yang, in the Department of Environmental Sciences at the University of Virginia (UVA). Previously, I earned my MSc with Distinction in Ecological Applications from Imperial College London and received a First-Class Honours BSc in Environmental Science from the University of Liverpool and Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University. Before coming to UVA, I spent one year as a research assistant in Prof. Jin Wu’s group at the University of Hong Kong.

My independent research began in plant physiology, where I conducted laboratory experiments to investigate plant responses to environmental stress, particularly heavy metal and microplastic pollution, supervised by Prof. Linxi Yuan during my undergraduate studies. Then, I expanded my research to modelling tree ecophysiology and seasonal dynamics at larger scales, including spring leaf phenology and optimality-based photosynthetic models.

Currently, my research interests broadly span anything related to plant and forest ecology, terrestrial ecosystems, remote sensing, and AI for environmental observation. I combine LiDAR, remote sensing, field measurements, and statistical/machine-learning approaches to translate complex environmental data into interpretable ecological information and uncover the mechanisms underlying observed patterns. My recent work focuses on using terrestrial and airborne LiDAR (TLS/ALS) to understand forest structure and function across spatial and temporal scales.

I’d love to connect with and learn from people working on similar problems. Feel free to reach out by email.
